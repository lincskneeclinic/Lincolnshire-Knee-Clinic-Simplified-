## 1. Purpose

This document defines the brand positioning, logo usage, tone of voice and communication rules for Lincolnshire Knee Clinic.

The purpose of the brand is to present Lincolnshire Knee Clinic as a consultant-led, clinically credible, patient-centred specialist knee service.

This document should guide:

- Website copy
- Patient education content
- Blog articles
- Booking pages
- Clinic pages
- Email wording
- Future patient portal text
- AI-generated content
- Social and publishing material
- Developer and design decisions affecting communication
- Logo and visual brand use

The brand must remain clinically trustworthy, professional and accessible.

---

## 2. Brand Positioning

Lincolnshire Knee Clinic should be positioned as:

```text
A consultant-led specialist knee clinic providing clear, evidence-aware information, assessment and treatment pathways for patients with knee pain, knee arthritis, sports knee injuries, injection needs and knee replacement concerns.
```

The brand should communicate:

- Specialist knee expertise
- Consultant-led clinical care
- Clear patient education
- Balanced treatment information
- Accessible private care pathways
- Safe urgent advice routing
- Professional governance
- Future-ready digital care

The brand should not be positioned as:

- A general wellness brand
- A physiotherapy-only service
- A fitness or performance app
- A cosmetic private clinic
- An urgent care provider
- A guaranteed outcome service
- A sales-led medical website
- A technology company replacing clinical care

## 4. Logo

The Lincolnshire Knee Clinic logo is a stylised abstract **“K”** mark using a teal and blue gradient.

The logo should be used as the primary visual brand mark for Lincolnshire Knee Clinic.

### 4.1 Primary Logo File

The approved logo file should be stored in the repository at:

```text
assets/brand/lkc-logo-k-transparent.png
```

### 4.2 Logo Description

The logo consists of:

- A stylised abstract “K”
- Teal and blue gradient forms
- Curved, flowing shapes
- A modern clinical visual tone
- A transparent background

The logo should support the brand qualities of:

- Clinical professionalism
- Modernity
- Clarity
- Trust
- Specialist focus
- Calm visual confidence

### 4.3 Logo Usage

The logo should be used:

- In the website header
- In the website footer
- On booking pages
- On legal/governance pages
- On patient education templates
- On future patient portal login screens
- On future patient portal dashboard screens
- On official clinic documents where appropriate
- On patient-facing digital materials

### 4.4 Logo Lockup

The logo should usually be paired with the full clinic name:

```text
Lincolnshire Knee Clinic
```

Preferred lockup:

```text
[Logo mark] Lincolnshire Knee Clinic
```

The logo mark may be used alone only where space is limited, such as:

- Favicon
- App-style icon
- Small mobile header
- Portal dashboard icon
- Social media profile image

### 4.5 Logo Clear Space

The logo should have adequate clear space around it.

Avoid placing the logo too close to:

- Page edges
- Text blocks
- Buttons
- Navigation items
- Other logos
- Photographs
- Clinical diagrams

As a practical rule, leave clear space at least equal to the width of the logo’s thickest stroke around the mark.

### 4.6 Logo Backgrounds

Preferred backgrounds:

- White
- Off-white
- Very pale clinical blue
- Soft blue-grey
- Dark navy only if contrast remains strong

Avoid placing the logo on:

- Busy photography
- Low-contrast backgrounds
- Bright colours
- Strong gradients
- Patterned surfaces
- Red or warning-coloured backgrounds

### 4.7 Logo Restrictions

The logo must not be:

- Distorted
- Stretched
- Squashed
- Rotated
- Cropped
- Recoloured without approval
- Placed inside heavy shadows
- Given glow effects
- Used with decorative filters
- Placed on low-contrast backgrounds
- Recreated manually
- Used as a watermark over clinical content
- Combined with unapproved slogans

### 4.8 Logo and Text Relationship

When paired with the clinic name, the text should feel:

- Professional
- Clear
- Legible
- Balanced with the logo mark
- Consistent with the approved typography

The clinic name should not be set in overly decorative, playful or casual fonts.

Preferred text style:

```text
Lincolnshire Knee Clinic
```

Avoid:

```text
LKC
```

as the primary public-facing name unless the full name has already been introduced.

## 5. Brand Values

### 5.1 Clinical Expertise

The clinic is led by specialist orthopaedic knowledge and consultant-level judgement.

Communication should demonstrate expertise through clarity, structure and accuracy rather than exaggerated claims.

### 5.2 Integrity

The clinic should communicate honestly about:

- Benefits
- Risks
- Limitations
- Alternatives
- Recovery expectations
- When treatment may not be appropriate

### 5.3 Patient Understanding

The clinic should help patients understand their symptoms, conditions and options.

Content should reduce confusion, not increase anxiety.

### 5.4 Safety

The clinic must avoid unsafe self-diagnosis and must direct urgent symptoms to appropriate urgent services.

### 5.5 Accessibility

The clinic should be understandable to patients with different levels of health literacy and digital confidence.

### 5.6 Professionalism

The brand should feel calm, structured and clinically mature.

It should not feel casual, gimmicky, sensational or overly commercial.

### 5.7 Future Readiness

The brand should support future patient portal functionality, digital workflows and AI-assisted content while maintaining clinical governance.

## 6. Tone of Voice

The Lincolnshire Knee Clinic tone should be:

- Professional
- Calm
- Clear
- Direct
- Evidence-aware
- Reassuring but not overpromising
- Patient-centred
- Consultant-led
- Clinically grounded
- Suitable for UK private healthcare

The tone should avoid being:

- Overly casual
- Sales-heavy
- Fear-based
- Overly technical
- Vague
- Promotional
- Exaggerated
- Robotic
- Dismissive
- Alarmist

## 7. Writing Principles

### 7.1 Be Clear

Use plain English wherever possible.

Better:

```text
Knee arthritis can cause pain, stiffness and difficulty walking.
```

Avoid:

```text
Degenerative tibiofemoral pathology may result in biomechanical compromise and functional limitation.
```

Use technical terms only when clinically useful, and explain them.

---

### 7.2 Be Specific

Avoid vague phrases that could apply to any clinic.

Better:

```text
Consultant-led assessment and treatment for knee pain, knee arthritis, sports knee injuries and knee replacement concerns.
```

Avoid:

```text
World-class care for all your orthopaedic needs.
```

---

### 7.3 Be Balanced

Discuss risks, limitations and alternatives.

Better:

```text
A knee injection may help reduce pain for some patients, but the effect varies and it may not be suitable for everyone.
```

Avoid:

```text
Our injections will get you moving pain-free again.
```

---

### 7.4 Be Safe

Do not imply that website content can diagnose or treat the patient individually.

Better:

```text
This information can help you understand possible causes of knee pain. A clinical assessment is needed for individual advice.
```

Avoid:

```text
Use this page to find out exactly what is wrong with your knee.
```

---

### 7.5 Be Reassuring Without Overpromising

Better:

```text
Many knee problems can be improved with the right assessment and treatment plan.
```

Avoid:

```text
We will get you back to normal.
```

---

### 7.6 Be Respectful

Avoid language that blames patients or trivialises pain.

Better:

```text
Knee pain can affect walking, stairs, work, sleep and daily activities.
```

Avoid:

```text
Don’t let bad knees ruin your life.
```

Avoid:

- Best
- Leading
- World-class
- Guaranteed
- Cure
- Pain-free
- Permanent fix
- Revolutionary
- Miracle
- Cutting-edge, unless specifically justified
- State-of-the-art, unless specifically justified
- No-risk

- Symptom checker
- Diagnosis tool

Avoid unsupported statistics such as:

- 15+ years
- 1000+ patients
- 98% success rate
- Most advanced
- Number one
- Highest rated

unless formally verified and approved.

## 11. Clinical Claims Policy

All clinical claims must be:

- Accurate
- Evidence-aware
- Balanced
- Clinically reviewed
- Suitable for UK patients
- Free from exaggeration
- Clearly distinguished from individual medical advice

Clinical claims must not:

- Promise results
- Guarantee pain relief
- Claim superiority without evidence
- Overstate benefits
- Understate risks
- Present treatment as suitable for everyone
- Replace consultation

### 11.1 Safer Claim Examples

Use:

```text
This treatment may help reduce pain in selected patients.
```

Avoid:

```text
This treatment removes knee pain.
```

Use:

```text
Your suitability for treatment depends on your symptoms, examination findings, imaging and overall health.
```

Avoid:

```text
This treatment is suitable for anyone with knee pain.
```

Use:

```text
Recovery varies between patients.
```

Avoid:

```text
You will recover quickly.
```

## 12. Patient-Facing Content Rules

Patient-facing content should:

- Start with the patient’s problem
- Explain medical terms
- Use short paragraphs
- Use clear headings
- Provide practical next steps
- Explain when to seek urgent advice
- Link to related symptoms, conditions and treatments
- Include medical disclaimer where appropriate
- Avoid overwhelming detail early on

### 12.1 Recommended Page Introduction Pattern

For symptom, condition and treatment pages, use this pattern:

```text
[Condition/symptom/treatment] can affect [daily activity/function]. This page explains common causes, how it is assessed, treatment options and when to seek medical advice.
```

Example:

```text
Knee arthritis can cause pain, stiffness and difficulty with walking, stairs and daily activities. This page explains common symptoms, how arthritis is assessed, treatment options and when to seek medical advice.
```

- Clear
- Confident
- Concise
- Reassuring
- Specialist

Homepage should answer:

- What is the clinic?
- Who is it for?
- What knee problems are treated?
- How can patients book?
- Where are appointments available?
- Why should patients trust the clinic?

---

### 13.2 Symptoms Pages

Tone:

- Patient-friendly
- Educational
- Safety-aware
- Non-diagnostic

Symptoms pages must not be framed as a diagnostic tool.

Use:

```text
Find information by symptom.
```

Avoid:

```text
Check what is wrong with your knee.
```

---

### 13.3 Condition Pages

Tone:

- Clinically structured
- Balanced
- Educational
- Evidence-aware

Condition pages should explain:

- What the condition is
- Symptoms
- Causes
- Assessment
- Treatment options
- When to seek help
- Related information

---

### 13.4 Treatment Pages

Tone:

- Transparent
- Balanced
- Practical
- Consultant-led

Treatment pages must include:

- Who may benefit
- Who may not be suitable
- Risks
- Alternatives
- Recovery expectations
- Individual assessment requirement

---

### 13.5 Injection Pages

Tone:

- Balanced
- Cautious
- Practical
- Evidence-aware

Injection pages should avoid overstating benefit.

Use:

```text
The effect of injection treatment varies between patients.
```

Avoid:

```text
This injection will restore your knee function.
```

---

### 13.6 Booking Page

Tone:

- Simple
- Practical
- Administrative
- Reassuring

- What patients can book
- What happens after booking
- What information may be needed
- What to do for urgent symptoms

Do not call Microsoft Bookings a patient portal.

---

### 13.7 Contact Page

Tone:

- Clear
- Safe
- Administrative

The contact page must state that urgent clinical problems should not be submitted through the contact form.

---

### 13.8 Legal and Governance Pages

Tone:

- Clear
- Formal
- Readable
- Non-ambiguous

Legal text should be reviewed before publication.

---

### 13.9 Future Patient Portal

Tone:

- Clear
- Secure

- Calm

Portal text should use simple action-oriented instructions:

```text

View your appointment

Update your details
```

Preferred trust signals:

- GMC Specialist Register
- Consultant orthopaedic knee surgeon
- Hospital affiliations
- Clinical review metadata
- Last reviewed date
- Evidence and resources
- Medical disclaimer
- Privacy and data protection information

```text

```

unless verified and approved.

## 19. AI-Generated Content Rules

AI may assist with:

- Drafting outlines
- Creating page structures
- Suggesting patient-friendly explanations
- Rewriting for readability
- Generating FAQs
- Producing social post drafts
- Creating SEO metadata drafts
- Creating internal documentation

AI must not:

- Publish directly
- Invent clinical evidence
- Make unsupported claims
- Create fake clinician credentials
- Create fake testimonials
- Create false clinic details
- Provide individual diagnosis
- Replace clinical review
- Approve legal or privacy text
- Autogenerate urgent advice outside approved wording

All AI-generated clinical content must be reviewed before publication.

Good:


Poor:

```text
Get back to pain-free living today with our world-class treatments.
```

Good:

```text
Knee arthritis can cause pain, stiffness and difficulty with walking, stairs and daily activities. Treatment depends on your symptoms, X-ray findings, general health and personal goals.
```

Poor:

```text
Knee arthritis is easily fixed with the latest advanced treatments.
```

### 20.3 Injection Page

Good:

```text
A corticosteroid injection may help reduce inflammation and pain in selected patients. The effect varies, and injections are not suitable for every type of knee problem.

```

## 21. Social and Publishing Tone

For future social media, newsletters or blog summaries, the tone should remain:

- Educational
- Professional
- Measured
- Useful
- Clinically safe

Social content should not become:

- Clickbait
- Fear-based
- Overly promotional
- Sensational
- Dismissive of NHS care
- Aggressively competitive

Example acceptable social tone:

```text
Knee pain on stairs is common and can have several causes, including arthritis, kneecap problems and tendon irritation. Persistent or worsening symptoms should be assessed properly.
```

Avoid:

```text
Knee pain on stairs? You may be destroying your joints without knowing it.
```

## 23. Final Brand Statement

Lincolnshire Knee Clinic should communicate specialist knee expertise with clarity, restraint and clinical authority.

The brand should help patients understand their symptoms and options without exaggeration, fear or false certainty.

The desired impression is:

```text
This is a serious, consultant-led knee clinic that explains things clearly and helps patients take the right next step.
```

This branding document should be treated as the source of truth for logo use, tone of voice and patient-facing communication unless formally updated.
