## 1. Purpose

This document defines the content structure, clinical governance rules, SEO content standards and AI content constraints for Lincolnshire Knee Clinic.

The purpose is to ensure that all content is:

- Clinically accurate
- Patient-friendly
- Evidence-aware
- Safe
- Search-optimised without being manipulative
- Consistent with the Lincolnshire Knee Clinic brand
- Suitable for UK patients
- Structured for future scalability
- Reviewed before publication

This document applies to:

- Symptom pages
- Condition pages
- Treatment pages
- Injection pages
- Education and blog articles
- FAQs
- Booking content
- Clinic pages
- Legal and governance pages
- Future patient portal support content
- AI-generated content drafts

## 2. Core Content Principles

All Lincolnshire Knee Clinic content must follow these principles.

### 2.1 Clinical Accuracy

Content must be clinically accurate, balanced and reviewed before publication.

It must not:

- Invent facts
- Invent statistics
- Overstate benefits
- Understate risks
- Guarantee outcomes
- Replace clinical consultation
- Provide individual diagnosis
- Misrepresent evidence
- Present one treatment as universally superior

### 2.2 Patient Clarity

Content should be easy for patients to understand.

Use:

- Plain English
- Short paragraphs
- Clear headings
- Practical explanations
- Logical page structure
- Patient-centred examples

Avoid:

- Unexplained jargon
- Dense academic language
- Long unbroken text blocks
- Marketing exaggeration
- Fear-based wording

### 2.3 Safety

Content must clearly direct patients to urgent services when appropriate.

Urgent advice wording must remain consistent:

```text
Urgent symptoms? Read urgent advice.
```

Supporting wording:

```text
Lincolnshire Knee Clinic does not provide emergency medical care. For life-threatening symptoms call 999. For urgent non-life-threatening advice use NHS 111.
```

Content must not imply:

- 24/7 clinic monitoring
- Emergency care provision
- Immediate response to website enquiries
- Diagnosis through website pages
- Online triage unless a governed triage system exists

### 2.4 Balanced Decision-Making

Content should support shared decision-making.

Treatment and injection pages must explain:

- Who may benefit
- Who may not be suitable
- Risks
- Benefits
- Limitations
- Alternatives
- Recovery expectations
- Need for individual assessment

### 2.5 Consistency

All content must align with:

```text
docs/project-vision.md
docs/sitemap.md
docs/design-system.md
docs/branding.md
```

## 4. Symptom Page Structure

Symptom pages help patients find information based on what they are experiencing.

They must not be presented as a diagnostic tool.

Use:

```text
Find information by symptom
```

Avoid:

```text
Symptom checker
Diagnosis tool
Find out exactly what is wrong
```

### 4.1 Approved Symptom Page Template

Each symptom page should include:

```text
1. Page title
2. Short patient-friendly summary
3. Common causes
4. When to seek urgent advice
5. How the symptom is assessed
6. Possible investigations
7. Treatment options
8. Related conditions
9. Related treatments
10. Related education articles
11. Booking call-to-action
12. Medical disclaimer
13. Clinical governance metadata
```

### 4.2 Symptom Page Example Title

```text
Knee Pain
```

### 4.3 Symptom Page Example Introduction

```text
Knee pain can affect walking, stairs, work, sleep and daily activities. It can have several causes, including arthritis, meniscal tears, ligament injuries, kneecap problems and inflammation. This page explains common causes, how knee pain is assessed and when to seek medical advice.
```

### 4.4 Symptom Page Safety Rule

Every symptom page should include a section titled:

```text
When to seek urgent medical advice
```

This section should direct patients to urgent care pathways where appropriate.


## 6. Treatment Page Structure

Treatment pages explain non-operative and operative management options.

### 6.1 Approved Treatment Page Template

Each treatment page should include:

```text
1. Page title
2. Short summary
3. What is the treatment?
4. Who may benefit?
5. Who may not be suitable?
6. Benefits
7. Risks and limitations
8. Alternatives
9. What happens before treatment
10. What happens during treatment
11. Recovery timeline
12. Follow-up
13. Frequently asked questions
14. Related conditions
15. Related education articles
16. Booking call-to-action
17. Medical disclaimer
18. Clinical governance metadata
```

### 6.2 Treatment Page Example Introduction

```text
Total knee replacement may be considered for patients with severe knee arthritis when pain and stiffness continue despite non-surgical treatment. Suitability depends on symptoms, examination findings, X-rays, general health and personal goals.
```

### 6.3 Treatment Content Rules

Treatment pages must not:

- Guarantee results
- Downplay complications
- Suggest treatment is suitable for everyone
- Use phrases such as “permanent cure”
- Overstate speed of recovery
- Claim superiority without evidence

Use:

```text
may help
may be considered
can be suitable for selected patients
depends on individual assessment
recovery varies between patients
```

Avoid:

```text
will cure
guaranteed results
pain-free
risk-free
best treatment
permanent fix
```

### 7.1 Approved Injection Page Template

Each injection page should include:

```text
13. Frequently asked questions

15. Related education articles
```

### 7.2 Injection Page Example Introduction

```text
A corticosteroid injection may help reduce inflammation and pain in selected knee conditions. The effect varies between patients, and injections are not suitable for every knee problem. A clinical assessment is needed to decide whether an injection is appropriate.
```

### 7.3 Injection Content Rules

Injection pages must:

- Explain variable response
- Explain limitations
- Include risks and side effects
- Avoid implying guaranteed pain relief
- Avoid overstating duration of benefit
- Compare options carefully
- Avoid presenting newer treatments as automatically superior

## 9. FAQ Structure

FAQs should be used to support clarity, not to hide important content.

### 9.1 FAQ Rules

FAQs should:

- Use real patient questions
- Provide short answers
- Link to deeper content where needed
- Avoid creating new clinical claims not covered elsewhere
- Avoid duplicating large sections of page content

### 9.2 Example FAQ Format

```text
Question: Can knee arthritis be treated without surgery?

Answer: Many patients with knee arthritis can be managed without surgery, especially in the earlier stages. Treatment may include activity modification, physiotherapy, weight management, pain relief, braces or injections. Surgery may be considered if symptoms remain severe despite non-surgical treatment.
```

## 15. Evidence and Sources

Clinical content should be evidence-aware and should link to reputable sources where appropriate.

### 15.1 Preferred Source Types

```text
NICE guidance
NHS patient information
Peer-reviewed papers
Relevant orthopaedic society guidance
Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency information where relevant
Product information where relevant to injection treatments
```

### 15.2 Evidence Rules

Content must not:

- Invent citations
- Misrepresent sources
- Overstate the certainty of evidence
- Claim evidence exists where it does not
- Use weak evidence to make strong claims
- Present opinion as fact without context

### 15.3 Evidence Language

Use:

```text
Evidence suggests
Some patients may
This may help
The effect varies
Research is still developing
Your suitability depends on assessment
```

Avoid:

```text
Evidence proves
Guaranteed
Always works
No risk
Best option
Superior treatment
```

## 16. SEO Content Rules

SEO should support clinical clarity, not replace it.

### 16.1 SEO Principles

Each page should have:

```text
One primary topic
Clear page title
Search-intent aligned introduction
Descriptive headings
Internal links
Readable URL
Meta title
Meta description
Relevant related pages
```

### 16.2 SEO Must Not

SEO must not lead to:

- Keyword stuffing
- Duplicate pages
- Doorway pages
- Overpromising titles
- Misleading local claims
- Fake comparison pages
- Excessive repetition of “best knee surgeon”
- Content written for search engines rather than patients

### 16.3 Approved SEO Content Approach

Use symptom-led, condition-led and treatment-led pages to match patient search behaviour.

Examples:

```text
/symptoms/knee-pain-on-stairs
/conditions/knee-arthritis
/treatments/total-knee-replacement
/injections/hyaluronic-acid-injection
/education/knee-replacement/how-long-does-knee-replacement-last
```


Meta titles should be:

- Clear
- Specific
- Not exaggerated
- Under approximately 60 characters where possible
- Relevant to the page topic

Example:

```text
Knee Arthritis Treatment in Lincolnshire
```

Avoid:

```text
Best Knee Arthritis Treatment Lincolnshire | Guaranteed Results
```

### 19.3 Meta Description Rules

Meta descriptions should be:

- Patient-friendly
- Accurate
- Approximately 150–160 characters where possible
- Free from guarantees
- Clear about page content

Example:

```text
Learn about knee arthritis symptoms, assessment and treatment options, including physiotherapy, injections and knee replacement.
```


## 23. Readability Standards

Content should be written for patients, not clinicians.

### 23.1 Readability Rules

Use:

- Short sentences
- Short paragraphs
- Plain English
- Clear headings
- Bullet points where helpful
- Definitions for medical terms
- Practical examples

Avoid:

- Long dense paragraphs
- Unexplained abbreviations
- Complex surgical detail early on
- Overuse of Latin or technical anatomical terms
- Excessive academic citations in the main text

### 23.2 Explaining Medical Terms

Use this pattern:

```text
A meniscus is a shock-absorbing cartilage structure inside the knee.
```

Avoid:

```text
Meniscal fibrocartilage pathology is a common source of mechanical symptoms.
```

## 24. Content Maintenance

Clinical content should be reviewed regularly.

### 24.1 Review Frequency

Suggested review cycle:

```text
Core clinical pages: every 12 months
Injection pages: every 6–12 months
Legal/privacy pages: whenever requirements change, and at least annually
Education articles: every 12–24 months depending on topic
Urgent advice wording: whenever service model or national advice changes
```

### 24.2 Review Triggers

Content should be reviewed when:

- Clinical guidance changes
- Services change
- Clinic locations change
- Booking process changes
- New treatments are added
- Evidence changes
- Safety concerns are identified
- Legal or privacy requirements change
- Patient feedback indicates confusion

---

## 25. Final Content Statement

Lincolnshire Knee Clinic content should help patients understand knee problems and treatment options clearly and safely.

The content must support clinical trust through accuracy, balance, restraint and governance.

The aim is not to produce the loudest or most promotional website. The aim is to produce a clinically credible, patient-friendly and search-visible digital platform that helps patients take the right next step.
