# Project Vision

## Lincolnshire Knee Clinic Digital Platform

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Approved project direction  
**Repository:** `lincskneeclinic/Lincolnshire-Knee-Clinic`  
**Primary domain:** `lincolnshirekneeclinic.co.uk`  
**Secondary domain:** `lincolnshirekneeclinic.com` redirecting to `.co.uk`

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Lincolnshire Knee Clinic is being developed as a consultant-led digital platform for patients with knee pain, knee arthritis, sports knee injuries, meniscal pathology, ligament injuries, injection needs, and knee replacement concerns.

The platform will initially function as a public clinical website and patient education resource. Over time, it will expand into a structured digital clinic ecosystem with online booking, clinic information, secure patient workflows, outcome tracking, rehabilitation support, and future patient portal functionality.

The website should act as the digital front door for the clinic, helping patients understand their symptoms, conditions, treatment options, injection choices, clinic locations, and booking routes.

The long-term ambition is to create a trusted, clinically governed, scalable digital knee clinic platform.

---

## 2. Vision Statement

To build a trusted digital knee clinic platform that provides clear, consultant-led information, improves access to specialist knee care, supports shared decision-making, and creates the foundation for a future secure patient portal.

Lincolnshire Knee Clinic should become a high-quality digital destination for patients seeking reliable information and specialist assessment for knee problems in Lincolnshire and surrounding areas.

---

## 3. Mission

The mission of Lincolnshire Knee Clinic is to:

- Educate patients with accurate and accessible knee health information
- Help patients understand symptoms, diagnoses and treatment options
- Provide clear pathways for private consultations, injections and follow-up care
- Support evidence-aware clinical decision-making
- Improve the patient journey before, during and after consultation
- Reduce administrative friction through structured digital workflows
- Build a future-ready system capable of supporting secure patient access, outcome tracking and rehabilitation tools

---

## 4. Strategic Objectives

### 4.1 Clinical Objectives

The platform should:

- Explain common knee symptoms in patient-friendly language
- Provide clinically accurate information about knee conditions
- Explain treatment options clearly, including risks, benefits and alternatives
- Provide balanced information about knee injections
- Support patients before consultation by improving understanding
- Encourage appropriate urgent medical attention where needed
- Avoid unsafe self-diagnosis
- Avoid exaggerated or unsupported clinical claims
- Reinforce consultant-led clinical governance

### 4.2 Patient Experience Objectives

The platform should:

- Be easy to navigate
- Be readable for middle-aged and elderly patients
- Work well on mobile devices
- Make booking simple
- Help patients find information by symptom, condition or treatment
- Provide clear clinic location information
- Explain what to expect before consultation
- Use calm, professional and non-alarming language
- Make urgent advice visible but not misleading

### 4.3 Business Objectives

The platform should:

- Establish Lincolnshire Knee Clinic as a specialist knee care provider
- Increase appropriate private consultation enquiries
- Support injection clinic enquiries
- Improve visibility for knee arthritis, knee replacement, meniscal injury and sports knee injury searches
- Provide scalable content infrastructure for education and blog articles
- Support future digital services without requiring a complete rebuild
- Provide a professional digital presence aligned with consultant-led private practice

### 4.4 Technical Objectives

The platform should:

- Use a modern, maintainable web architecture
- Support structured content
- Support future database-backed features
- Allow phased development
- Maintain source control through GitHub
- Support future Supabase integration
- Support Microsoft Bookings and Teams integration in Phase 1
- Allow later patient portal development with appropriate security and governance
- Avoid premature complexity during the first public website phase

## 5. Target Users

### 5.1 Primary Patients

The main patient groups are:

- Adults with knee arthritis
- Patients considering knee replacement
- Patients with persistent knee pain
- Patients seeking knee injections
- Patients with meniscal tears
- Patients with ligament injuries
- Patients with sports-related knee injuries
- Patients with pain after previous knee surgery or knee replacement

### 5.2 Typical Age Groups

The platform should primarily serve:

- Patients aged 45–80 with degenerative knee problems, arthritis and knee replacement questions
- Patients aged 20–40 with sports injuries, meniscal tears or ligament injuries
- Relatives or carers helping older patients navigate care options

### 5.3 Referrers and Professional Users

Secondary users may include:

- General practitioners
- Physiotherapists
- Sports therapists
- Private hospitals
- Insurers
- Administrative staff
- Clinical colleagues

## 6. Core Principles

### 6.1 Clinical Accuracy

All clinical content must be accurate, balanced and evidence-aware.

Clinical information should not:

- Exaggerate outcomes
- Guarantee success
- Replace consultation
- Provide individual diagnosis
- Use unsupported superiority claims
- Invent evidence or statistics

### 6.2 Patient First

The platform must prioritise patient understanding.

Content should be:

- Clear
- Structured
- Calm
- Readable
- Practical
- Relevant to real patient concerns

### 6.3 Simplicity

The website should be simple to use.

Patients should be able to quickly find:

- What their symptom may mean
- What condition they may have
- What treatments may be available
- Where clinics are located
- How to book an appointment
- What to do if symptoms are urgent

### 6.4 Trust

Trust should be built through:

- Consultant-led positioning
- Professional registration information
- Hospital affiliation information
- Clear disclaimers
- Transparent clinical review dates
- Evidence-aware content
- Safe urgent advice routing
- Avoidance of sales-heavy language

### 6.5 Accessibility

The platform should be usable by patients with varying levels of digital confidence.

Design and content should support:

- Older patients
- Mobile users
- Patients with visual limitations
- Patients who are anxious or in pain
- Patients seeking clear step-by-step information

### 6.6 Scalability

The system should be designed so that future features can be added without rebuilding the platform from scratch.

Future expansion may include:

- Patient portal
- Questionnaires
- Outcome scores
- Document access
- Rehabilitation tracking
- Appointment history
- Secure messaging if approved
- Administrative dashboards

## 7. Platform Scope

The platform will be developed in phases.

### 7.1 Phase 1 Scope — Public Website

Phase 1 should include:

- Home page
- About page
- Symptoms hub
- Conditions hub
- Treatments hub
- Injections hub
- Education & Blog hub
- Clinics page
- Booking page
- Contact page
- Urgent advice page
- Legal and governance pages

Phase 1 should use:

- Microsoft Bookings for appointment scheduling
- Outlook for calendar management
- Microsoft Teams for video consultations if used
- Public website content for patient education
- GitHub as source of truth for documentation and code

Phase 1 should not include:

- Full patient portal
- Open patient self-registration
- Secure messaging
- Patient document storage
- Medical record access
- Outcome score collection unless properly governed

### 7.2 Phase 2 Scope — Content Expansion

Phase 2 should include:

- Full condition library
- Full treatment library
- Full injection pages
- Patient education articles
- FAQs
- SEO metadata
- Internal linking
- Content governance workflow
- Author/reviewer metadata
- Last reviewed dates

### 7.3 Phase 3 Scope — Patient Portal Architecture

Phase 3 should design the technical and visual foundation for:

- Secure login
- Invite-only account activation
- Multi-factor authentication
- Patient dashboard
- Appointments
- Documents
- Questionnaires
- Outcome scores
- Rehabilitation tracking

This phase is architectural and planning-focused.

### 7.4 Phase 4 Scope — Secure Patient Portal Implementation

Phase 4 may implement:

- Supabase authentication
- Role-based access control
- Patient records
- Appointment data
- Document access
- Outcome score tracking
- Rehabilitation tracking
- Administrative dashboard
- Audit logs
- Secure messaging if clinically and operationally approved

### 9.1 Content Types

The platform should support:

- Symptom pages
- Condition pages
- Treatment pages
- Injection pages
- Education articles
- Patient guides
- FAQs
- Clinic pages
- Legal/governance pages
- Future portal support content

### 9.2 Content Standards

All clinical content should include:

- Clear title
- Plain-language overview
- Structured sections
- Evidence-aware explanations
- Risks and limitations where appropriate
- Related symptoms
- Related conditions
- Related treatments

- Calm

## 12. Microsoft 365 Role

Microsoft 365 should support operational workflows but should not become the long-term application backend.

Appropriate Microsoft 365 uses include:

- Microsoft Bookings for appointment booking
- Outlook for email and calendar communication
- Microsoft Teams for video consultation
- OneDrive or SharePoint for internal documents
- Power Automate for administrative workflows

The long-term clinical application backend should be designed separately, likely using Supabase or a similar platform with appropriate healthcare data governance.

Possible portal modules include:

- Login
- Invite-only account activation
- Multi-factor authentication
- Dashboard
- Appointments
- Documents

## 14. Data Protection and Governance

The platform must recognise that healthcare data is sensitive.

Future secure functionality must consider:

- UK GDPR
- Data Protection Act 2018
- Special category health data
- Consent
- Data minimisation
- Data retention
- Role-based access control
- Multi-factor authentication
- Audit trails
- Incident response
- Secure document access
- Clear patient privacy information

Public website contact forms should not encourage patients to submit urgent clinical problems or excessive sensitive medical information.

Legal, privacy and governance pages must be reviewed before publication.

---

## 15. Urgent Advice Position

Lincolnshire Knee Clinic does not provide emergency medical care.

Urgent advice wording should be safe, clear and consistent.

Approved short wording:

```text
Urgent symptoms? Read urgent advice.
```

Approved supporting wording:

```text
Lincolnshire Knee Clinic does not provide emergency medical care. For life-threatening symptoms call 999. For urgent non-life-threatening advice use NHS 111.
```

Urgent advice must not imply:

- 24/7 clinic availability
- Emergency care provision
- Immediate clinical response from the clinic
- Monitoring of website enquiries for urgent problems
