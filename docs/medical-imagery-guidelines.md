# Lincolnshire Knee Clinic – Medical Imagery Guidelines

## Purpose

This document defines the standards for all medical images, diagrams, illustrations, annotated scans, and patient-facing visuals used across the Lincolnshire Knee Clinic website.

It exists to ensure:
- consistent visual quality
- medically accurate educational imagery
- safe and lawful use of licensed or generated content
- accessibility across desktop, tablet, and mobile
- clear placement of visuals within symptoms, conditions, treatments, injections, recovery, diagnostics, and clinic pages

This file must be consulted before generating, selecting, placing, or modifying medical imagery for the website.

---

## Core Principles

All website visuals must be:

1. **Patient-facing**
   - clear, calm, reassuring
   - educational rather than dramatic
   - easy to interpret by non-medical users

2. **Clinically credible**
   - anatomically accurate
   - visually realistic where appropriate
   - reviewed for clinical plausibility before publication

3. **Consistent**
   - similar colour palette
   - similar illustration style
   - similar labelling logic
   - similar spacing and layout treatment

4. **Accessible**
   - readable on desktop, iPad, and mobile
   - not dependent on tiny labels
   - not reliant on hover-only interaction
   - usable with keyboard and touch

5. **Reusable**
   - based on a system, not one-off unrelated images
   - applicable across multiple conditions and treatment pages

## AI-Generated Visuals Policy

AI-generated visuals are permitted for patient education purposes if all of the following are true:

- they are used as illustrations, not as actual patient scans
- they are clinically reviewed before publication
- they do not claim to show a real patient unless they do
- they do not falsely represent diagnostic evidence
- they are not misleading, sensational, or anatomically implausible

AI visuals are especially suitable for:
- realistic 2D medical illustrations
- anatomy diagrams
- injection explanation diagrams
- comparison images
- simplified MRI/X-ray educational examples
- pathway graphics

AI visuals must not be used to imply:
- diagnosis of a specific patient
- guaranteed outcomes
- actual operative images when they are not

## Preferred Style

### Brand Tone
- calm
- premium
- clinically authoritative
- modern
- clean
- not cartoonish
- not overly glossy or “tech demo”
- not generic stock-photo style

### Preferred Visual Direction
Use:
- realistic 2D medical illustrations
- clean white or very light backgrounds
- restrained colours
- subtle shadows
- soft contrast
- clear anatomy
- premium patient-education quality

Avoid:
- hyper-saturated images
- dramatic blood-heavy surgical scenes
- unrealistic cartoon anatomy
- cluttered infographic noise
- inconsistent visual styles across pages
- unrelated image styles on adjacent pages

### Website Colour Fit
Visuals should work with the website palette:
- dark navy / deep blue sections
- soft pale blue content panels
- teal accents
- white and light grey backgrounds

Images should not clash with this palette.

## Standard Master Prompt Style

Use the following master style phrasing when generating medical illustrations:

> Anatomically accurate, realistic 2D medical illustration, premium patient-education style, clean white background, clinically credible, restrained natural colours, soft medical lighting, no labels, no text, no watermark, no logo, no blood, non-graphic, modern orthopaedic educational style.

This style block should be reused across most medical illustration prompts.

## Default Prompt Templates

### 1. Anatomy Illustration Template
> Anatomically accurate, realistic 2D medical illustration of the adult human knee, [view], showing [structures]. Premium patient-education style, clinically accurate anatomy, white background, restrained colours, soft shadows, no labels, no text, no watermark, no logo, modern orthopaedic educational style.

### 2. Pathology Illustration Template
> Anatomically accurate, realistic 2D medical illustration of a knee with [condition]. Show [specific pathology details]. Use a clean cutaway or three-quarter clinical view. Premium patient-education style, white background, restrained colours, no labels, no text, clinically accurate, no blood, no watermark, no logo.

### 3. Comparison Illustration Template
> Side-by-side realistic 2D medical illustration comparing [state A] and [state B]. Keep the same angle, scale, lighting, and anatomical proportions in both panels. Premium patient-education style, white background, no labels, no text, clinically accurate, restrained colours.

### 4. Injection Illustration Template
> Realistic 2D medical illustration showing a [type of injection] into the knee joint. Show accurate knee anatomy, safe needle position, and the target intra-articular space. Non-graphic, patient-friendly, premium educational style, white background, no labels, no text, no branding.

### 5. Treatment Procedure Illustration Template
> Realistic 2D medical illustration of [treatment/procedure], showing the relevant knee anatomy and the principle of treatment. Premium patient-education style, white background, clinically accurate, restrained colours, no labels, no text, no watermark, no logo.

### 6. Annotated Imaging Example Template
> Create an educational imaging example of [X-ray/MRI/ultrasound] showing [condition]. The image should be clear and realistic, with optional separate annotation overlays added later in the website or design software. Keep the underlying image clean, high contrast, patient-education oriented, and easy to understand.

## Interactivity Rules

Interactive diagrams are allowed only if they improve understanding and remain fully usable on touch devices.

### Good use of interactivity
- accordion anatomy labels
- click/tap hotspots
- expandable comparisons
- toggled before/after overlays
- tap-to-view annotations
- simple step pathways

### Bad use of interactivity
- hover-only labels
- tiny hotspot targets
- complex drag interfaces
- desktop-only interactions
- essential information hidden behind interaction that fails on mobile

### Mobile and Tablet Rule
If an interactive visual becomes too dense on mobile:
- replace it with a simplified static version
- or show a reduced layout with a “view larger” option

Desktop may use richer visuals.  
Mobile must use simpler alternatives if needed.

---

## Accessibility Rules

All visuals must support accessibility best practice.

### Required
- descriptive alt text
- adequate contrast
- no critical meaning conveyed only by colour
- mobile readability
- no tiny labels
- no hover-only dependencies
- no fast animation

### Alt Text Standard
Alt text should describe:
- what the image shows
- what condition or treatment is illustrated
- what educational point it supports

Example:
> Illustration comparing a healthy knee joint with a knee affected by osteoarthritis, showing cartilage loss and narrowing of the medial joint space.

---

## Labelling Rules

### Preferred method
Do not generate labels inside AI artwork when avoidable.

Instead:
- generate clean label-free images
- add labels afterwards in Figma, Canva, SVG, or website components

Reason:
- AI-generated labels are often misspelled
- label placement is inconsistent
- accessibility is easier when labels are added separately

### If labels are needed
Use:
- large readable text
- short clinical labels
- consistent label style
- non-overlapping leader lines

---

## Realism Rules

Use realism carefully.

### Suitable realism
- anatomically accurate 2D medical realism
- clinically clean surgical concepts
- realistic anatomy and pathology

### Avoid
- photorealistic gore
- disturbing intra-operative detail
- misleading scan realism presented as real patient imaging
- sensational surgical imagery

Patient-facing realism should reassure rather than alarm.

## Licensing Rules

Before using any non-generated visual:
1. confirm source
2. confirm licence
3. confirm commercial reuse permission
4. confirm attribution requirements
5. store source details where needed

### Attribution Storage
Maintain a simple attribution record for any non-AI third-party asset used on the website.

Recommended file:
`/docs/image-attributions.md`

Each record should include:
- image file name
- source URL
- author/source
- licence
- attribution text
- page used on

- source checked
